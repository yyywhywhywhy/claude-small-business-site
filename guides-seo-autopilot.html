Step 3 · Generate a Shopify access token

This is what lets Claude publish to your store. Seven clicks in the Shopify admin:

  1. 1Log in to your Shopify admin
  2. 2Settings → Apps and sales channels → Develop apps
  3. 3Click Create an app and name it anything — Claude SEO works fine
  4. 4Open the app → Configure Admin API scopes
  5. 5Tick write_content and read_content
  6. 6Click Save, then Install app
  7. 7Click Reveal token once and copy it immediately — you only get one look
⚠Treat the token like a password. It stays in your local project folder. Never commit it to GitHub, never paste it into a shared document, and add it to .gitignore before you push anything.

The workflow · Steps 4–8

ℹThis is the full run for every new article. Do it manually the first time so you can see what each step produces. Once it's familiar, the automation prompt at the end collapses all of it into one message.

Start Claude Code:

4

Find keyword opportunities

Keyword research

Claude reads your competitors' blogs, finds the topics they cover thinly, and comes back with five directions a new site can realistically win.

Prompt 1 · fill the brackets, then send 
I sell [your product] on Shopify. My target market is [target market].

Please analyse the blog content on these competitors:
- [competitor blog URL 1]
- [competitor blog URL 2]

I want to know:
1. Which topics and keyword areas do their blogs cover?
2. Which topics are they covering but not in enough depth?
3. Based on those gaps, recommend 5 blog keywords suited to a new site

Criteria:
- Informational search intent, not transactional
- Long tail, relatively low competition
- Directly related to what I sell
5

Narrow to one

Keyword filter

Three criteria decide which of the five is worth your first article.

CriterionThresholdWhy
Keyword difficulty

Ranking first for something nobody searches for gets you nothing.
Search intent
Informational

💡Claude has no search volume data. It's good at generating and clustering candidates — it cannot tell you real numbers, and will produce plausible-looking ones if pushed. Check volume and difficulty in Google Keyword Planner, which is free.
Prompt 2 · send as is 
From the 5 keywords you gave me, filter further using these criteria:

1. Keyword difficulty under 30 — what a new site can realistically compete for
2. Monthly search volume above 100 — enough traffic to be worth writing for
3. Informational search intent, not transactional

Pick the single best keyword for my first blog post, and explain why you chose it.
6

Teach it your voice

Brand voice

Feed Claude your actual writing before it drafts anything. The more you give it, the closer the output lands to how your brand really sounds.

⚠Don't skip this step. Articles written without it open with “In today's fast-paced world…” and readers leave immediately. Google isn't penalising you for using AI — it's responding to the fact that nobody finishes the page. And don't run the output through an AI humaniser; it only makes the writing worse.
Prompt 3 · fill the brackets, then send 
Before we write anything, here is our brand voice.

[Describe your style. For example:
We sell XX. Our tone is relaxed and down to earth.
We focus on real everyday situations and avoid
sounding clinical or corporate.]

Reference writing:
[Paste things you have already published so Claude can learn
how you sound. Give at least 3 to 5 passages from different
contexts: product descriptions, Instagram captions,
customer emails, your about page.]

Remember this voice and use it for everything we write from here.
💡More material is always better. Product pages, social captions, emails you've actually sent, your about page — anything written in your own voice sharpens the result.
7

Write the article

Article generation

A full post against the chosen keyword — structure, meta fields and an internal link, in your voice.

Prompt 4 · fill the brackets, then send 
Now write a complete SEO blog post.

Target keyword: [the keyword from Step 5]
Audience: [target market and who they are]
Voice: the brand voice I gave you above

Structure:
- SEO title containing the keyword, under 60 characters
- Meta description containing the keyword, under 150 characters
- One H1
- 3 to 4 H2 sections, 150 to 200 words each
- At least one H3 under every H2
- One internal link near the end, pointing to my product page
  URL: [your product page URL]
- 800 to 1000 words total

Rules:
- No generic opening line
- Open with a real situation the reader will recognise
- Do not invent product specs, statistics or research figures
✓Check three things before moving on: the meta title contains the keyword and fits in 60 characters, the H2s answer the reader's question before mentioning your product, and the internal link points at the right page.
8

Publish to Shopify

Publish

Claude calls the Shopify API, creates the blog if it doesn't exist, and drops the article in as a draft.

Prompt 5 · fill the brackets, then send 
Two things please.

First, create a new blog on my Shopify store called
[blog name, for example: Tips and Guides].

Second, publish the article we just wrote to that blog,
as a draft.

Article details:
- title: the SEO title you generated
- body_html: the article, converted to HTML
- tags: 2 or 3, based on the keyword
- meta title: the SEO title
- meta description: the meta description

When it is done, give me the draft URL in the Shopify admin.
⚠Check the draft before you publish it. Two things specifically: any product facts in the article — sizes, materials, certifications — and whether the internal link points at the right product page. Then hit publish.

Automate it · set up once

Once you've been through the full run, this configuration turns every future article into three commands.

9
Generate your CLAUDE.md
💡What CLAUDE.md does. Claude Code looks for this file every time it starts and reads it automatically — think of it as the handbook you write once for a new hire. Your store details, brand voice, SEO thresholds and competitor list stop being things you retype. It also holds a used keywords list that updates after every publish, so you never accidentally write the same article twice.
Prompt 6 · send after your first full run 
Based on our conversation today, create a CLAUDE.md file in the project root.

Include:
1. Store details — domain, blog handle
2. Product details — category, target market, product page URLs
3. Brand voice — tone plus the reference writing I gave you
4. SEO rules — difficulty, volume and intent thresholds
5. Competitor sites
6. Used keywords — the keyword from today, with the date
7. Publishing setup — REST API

Claude Code will read this automatically every time it starts.
⚡The automation prompt

With CLAUDE.md in place, this single message runs the whole pipeline. It stops once — at keyword choice — and finishes everything else on its own.

Automation · one message per article 
Following CLAUDE.md, write and publish a new SEO blog post.

Steps:
1. Analyse competitor blogs for new keyword opportunities
2. Pick a keyword, skipping anything already in Used Keywords
3. Stop and wait for me to confirm the keyword
4. Write the full article — brand voice, full structure
5. Publish it to Shopify as a draft
6. Add the keyword and today's date to Used Keywords in CLAUDE.md

Pause at step 3 and wait for my confirmation before continuing.

05

Mistakes to avoid

01
Publishing raw AI output

Without the brand voice step, the writing is generic and readers bounce. Google isn't demoting you for using AI — it's responding to the fact that nobody stays on the page. Do Step 6 properly and this problem disappears.

02
Chasing keywords that are too broad

“best yoga mat” and “yoga mat benefits” are already owned by sites with years of authority. Hold the three criteria: difficulty under 30, volume above 100, informational intent.

03
Publishing thirty posts in a week

A sudden flood reads as a content farm. Two or three a week, consistently, is what works. Meaningful traffic usually starts somewhere after the fiftieth article — the rhythm matters more than the burst.

04
Not checking product facts

Claude doesn't know your real products. Dimensions, materials, certifications and care instructions can all come out wrong and sound completely confident. Read every draft before it goes live.

Common questions

Claude Code runs on your own machine and can read and write files, run commands and call APIs directly. The chat interface can only hand you text back. This pipeline needs a tool that can create files and publish to Shopify on its own, so Claude Code is the one to use.
The token lives in your local project folder and is used to call the Shopify API from your machine — it isn't uploaded anywhere. The real risk is you: don't push a file containing it to GitHub, and don't share your CLAUDE.md with anyone. Add it to .gitignore before your first commit.
Claude doesn't know your actual product details, and it will state incorrect specs with complete confidence. The draft step is your chance to catch that before a customer reads it. It costs two minutes and protects both your credibility and your rankings.
Longer than you'd like. A new site typically waits three to six months for rankings to move at all, and noticeable traffic tends to arrive somewhere after fifty to a hundred articles. The point of automating this is that consistency stops depending on your motivation — two or three posts a week, every week, without it eating your evenings.
Not on their main terms, and you shouldn't try. That's exactly what the difficulty threshold in Step 5 is for. Big sites tend to cover broad topics well and specific ones badly — the gaps Claude finds in Step 4 are where a small store can win. Take the narrow questions they've ignored.
One last thing

This is a starting point, not a formula. Every brand sells something different and cares about different things, so treat this as a skeleton to pull apart — change the criteria, rewrite the prompts, add the steps your business actually needs.

What matters is that you end up with a process you can repeat without thinking about it. This is just the version that worked for me.
